title: "Your portfolio is a conversation, not a trophy case"
description: "People don’t hire a list of tools. They hire judgment, and writing is where judgment shows."
date: "2026-01-18"

A portfolio full of logos and buzzwords looks busy. A portfolio that explains tradeoffs feels trustworthy.

When I look at work, my own or someone else’s, I’m less interested in the stack list and more interested in the decisions. What constraint shaped this? What almost went wrong? What would you change next time?

Those answers tell me how someone thinks under pressure. Tools change. Judgment travels.

That’s also why I write. Not to sound clever, but to leave a trail of how I’m learning. Writing forces you to finish a thought. Shipping forces you to finish a product. Both are practice for the same muscle: making something clear enough that another person can meet you there.

If your portfolio feels empty, start with one honest story about a hard problem. One conversation beats ten trophies.
