“Life is a race.”

From school rankings to college placements to job titles, we’ve been conditioned to measure progress by speed. Who moved first. Who achieved faster. Who reached higher.

Someone is switching companies every year. Someone is doubling their salary. Someone is building and launching their own products, speaking at conferences.

And without realizing it, you start running: not because you’re inspired, but because you’re afraid of being left behind.

The problem with living in a race mindset is that you stop asking why you’re running. You only focus on how fast.

In fast-moving fields like IT, it’s easy to confuse movement with growth. Learning five new tools in a month feels productive. Switching roles quickly feels ambitious. Saying yes to everything feels driven.

But real growth is often slower and less glamorous. It looks like mastering fundamentals deeply. It looks like understanding systems instead of shortcuts. It looks like building stability before chasing scale.

Because careers are not 100-meter sprints. They are long, unpredictable marathons. And in marathons, pacing matters more than speed.

The ones who last aren’t necessarily the fastest starters. They’re the ones who didn’t burn out trying to impress the crowd.

Maybe life feels like a race. But maybe the real win is building something steady enough that you don’t have to keep running in panic.

Not everything needs acceleration. Some things need direction.

And direction always beats speed.
